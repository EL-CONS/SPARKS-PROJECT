1. **Sparks** — We are Cosmonautas, Computer Science students in Hermosillo, and we built Sparks: every spark that moves has a fingerprint (`CONTEXTO.md`, `docs/pitch/pitch-3min-es.md`).
2. **Look twice** — In 1930, Clyde Tombaugh found Pluto by alternating two photos of the same sky taken six days apart on a blink comparator (`sparks-en-breve.md`).
3. **The sky in 102 colors** — The prior QR2+QR3 census in `data-sample/results.json` (provenance under review in `docs/auditoria/procedencia.md`) records `1,607,473` science images across 102 infrared bands (`data-new/results.json` analyzes `470` images across the four challenge fields), impossible to inspect by eye (`docs/pitch/pitch-3min-es.md`).
4. **Why it is hard** — A fast source moves only a few pixels per year across images taken in different wavelengths and with per-detector noise (`docs/pitch/pitch-3min-es.md`).
5. **We blink the sky** — Enter coordinates and Sparks aligns the epochs to show in blue where it was, in amber where it is now, and an arrow pointing where it is going (`sparks-en-breve.md`).
6. **Barnard matches Gaia** — On Barnard's Star, the prior run in `data-sample/results.json` (provenance under review in `docs/auditoria/procedencia.md`) measured `10.23 ± 0.43″/yr` vs. `10.39″/yr` from Gaia DR3 (`0.4σ`).
7. **WISE 0855, invisible to Gaia** — Sparks measures the coldest known brown dwarf (`6.04 ± 0.76″/yr`, `PA 289.1°`, `2.7σ` vs. `8.088″/yr`, and `D6/D2 >= 10.09` in `data-new/results.json`), and in Subtract view shows its cold signature dark at short wavelengths and bright at 4–5 µm (`CONTEXTO.md`, `docs/integracion/estado.md`).
8. **Honest validation** — In the prior frozen test (`final_test` in `data-sample/results.json`, provenance under review in `docs/auditoria/procedencia.md`) on 9 unseen fast-moving stars, the physical rule found `8/9` with `0` false alarms across `541` candidates, outperforming LightGBM (`6/9` with `10` false alarms); by object class, the v2 random forest reaches `78.3%` (`54/69` in `ml-clasificador/resultados_v2.json`).
9. **Real data and coverage** — Out of `36` test objects, `35` have at least two seasons separated by more than 60 days in `spherex_dr1` + `spherex_qr3` (Gliese 687 has 1 season with 965 images over 17 months), and all `36` have DR1 coverage (`221–965` images per object; `docs/sprint-13-30/cobertura-y-acceso.md`).
10. **Candidates to review, for everyone** — Sparks does not claim discoveries: it surfaces candidates to review from any browser or phone for volunteers, schools, and astronomers (`CONTEXTO.md`, `docs/pitch/pitch-3min-es.md`).
